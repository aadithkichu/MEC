### Question 1 (3 Marks)

**List out any six safety practices used with ladders in constructional sites.**

1. **Inspection & Condition:** Use ladders of sound material, good construction, and adequate strength; never use ladders with defective or missing rungs.


2. **Unpainted Wooden Ladders:** Do not paint wooden ladders, as paint can conceal structural defects.


3. **Stable Placement:** Never place a ladder on movable or insecure objects like boxes or barrels, and secure the base or station a worker at the bottom to prevent slipping.


4. **Landing Height:** Ensure ladders used for access extend at least 1 metre above the landing surface or provide an adequate handhold.


5. **Climbing Safety:** Always face the ladder while climbing or descending, and avoid carrying heavy, bulky loads or wearing slippery footwear.


6. **Electrical & Occupancy Precautions:** Never use metal ladders near exposed live electrical wires, and permit only one person at a time to work from a ladder.

### Question 6 (7 Marks)

**Discuss important types of ergonomic hazards associated with industries.**

Ergonomic hazards in industrial environments are physical conditions that increase the risk of Musculoskeletal Disorders (MSDs) and Cumulative Trauma Disorders (CTDs). The primary types of ergonomic hazards include:

1. **Awkward Posture:**
* Working in non-neutral positions such as stooping, bending, twisting, kneeling, or reaching overhead.


* Places excessive biomechanical strain on the spine, neck, shoulders, and knees. For instance, overhead drilling or manual floor finishing forces unnatural joint alignment and accelerates muscle fatigue.




2. **Heavy Lifting and High Forces:**
* Exerting excessive force during activities like manual lifting of materials exceeding 50 lbs, carrying, pushing, or pulling.


* Acute or repeated high force exertion is the leading cause of disabling back injuries, muscle tears, joint strain, and hernias.




3. **Repetitive Motion:**
* Performing identical movements repeatedly at high frequencies without sufficient recovery intervals between tasks.


* Continuous repetition (e.g., hammering, tying rebar, or manual caulking) causes wear and tear on tendons and nerve sheaths, resulting in conditions like tendonitis, tenosynovitis, and carpal tunnel syndrome.




4. **Static Posture:**
* Maintaining a fixed or constrained body posture over an extended period without movement.


* Reduces localized blood circulation, preventing the removal of metabolic waste products (like lactic acid) from muscles, leading to persistent muscle tightness and pain.




5. **Vibration and Contact Stress:**
* Prolonged use of high-vibration power tools or direct mechanical pressure from hard/sharp tool handles pressing against soft body tissues.


* Hand-arm vibration can impair blood circulation and cause neuro-vascular conditions like Raynaud's disease (white finger) or Hand-Arm Vibration Syndrome (HAVS).

### Question 7 (7 Marks)

**Explain any three critical hazards in demolition process. Also list any six safety measures to be ensured for demolition operation.**

#### **Three Critical Hazards in Demolition:**

1. **Unintentional Structural Collapse:** The unexpected, premature collapse of walls, floors, or the main structure due to structural instability, sequence failure, or undermining during demolition activities.


2. **Falls from Heights and Falling Objects:** Risk of workers falling through openings or from elevated surfaces, as well as injuries to personnel below caused by falling debris or structural members.


3. **Striking Live Services:** Accidental contact with or puncturing of overhead or underground utility services such as live electricity lines, gas mains, water pipes, or steam lines.



#### **Six Safety Measures for Demolition Operations:**

1. **Pre-Demolition Site Survey:** Conduct a comprehensive engineering survey prior to starting work to evaluate structural stability, plan the sequence, and locate scaffolds and protective screens.


2. **Utility Isolation:** Ensure all electric power, gas, water, and steam supply lines inside and outside the structure are shut off, disconnected, or safely controlled.


3. **Fencing and Warning Signs:** Securely fence and cordon off the entire demolition perimeter and display prominent warning boards to restrict unauthorized access and protect the public.


4. **Protection of Adjacent Structures:** Implement bracing, shoring, or underpinning to preserve the stability of nearby structures from collapse caused by ground movement or loss of support.


5. **Dust Control Measures:** Install wet suppression, water spraying, or enclosure systems to control airborne dust emissions generated during structural breakup.


6. **Debris Management & Segregation:** Methodically handle debris and segregate hazardous materials (e.g., chemical residues, toxic substances) from non-hazardous demolition waste.

### Question 2 (3 Marks)

**What is meant by point of operation?**

* **Definition:** The point of operation is the specific area on a machine where work is actively performed on the material, such as cutting, shaping, boring, or forming of stock.


* **Safety Significance:** Machine operators must frequently work close to this location, making it a high-risk area for caught-in or point-of-operation injuries.


* **Safeguarding:** Point-of-operation hazards are typically mitigated by installing physical machine guards or using optical sensing technologies like safety light curtains to prevent operator contact with dangerous moving parts.

### Question 3 (3 Marks)

**What are the three stages associated with preventive maintenance of chain slings?**

The three fundamental stages of preventive maintenance applied to material handling equipment like chain slings are:

1. **Inspection:** Conducting periodic examinations (at least once every 12 months by a competent person) to check for defects such as bent links, cracks, gouges, or link wear exceeding 10% of original dimensions.


2. **Repair / Replacement:** Taking damaged or overloaded chain slings out of service immediately and replacing defective links, bent master links, or deformed hooks.


3. **Overhaul & Storage:** Ensuring proper long-term maintenance by storing chain slings on designated racks to prevent mechanical/corrosive damage, avoiding chemical exposures (acidic/caustic solutions), and verifying temperature limits (-40°C to 200°C).

### Question 8 (7 Marks)

**Describe any four hazards associated with machinery and suggest any one preventive measure for each hazard.**

#### 1. Crushing Hazards

* **Description:** Crushing occurs when a worker or body part is struck by heavy machinery, caught between two moving parts, or trapped between a moving machine component and a fixed structure or wall.


* **Preventive Measure:** Ensure operators are trained on safe operating zones, maintain constant situational awareness, and use physical barriers or presence-sensing devices to prevent entry into crushing zones.



#### 2. Cutting and Severing Hazards

* **Description:** Caused by body contact with moving sharp edges (such as bandsaw blades), rotating components (such as lathe chucks or drill bits), or moving power tool parts that can sever skin and tissue.


* **Preventive Measure:** Install fixed or adjustable machine guards over blades and rotating parts, and ensure chucks/bits are firmly secured before starting the machine.



#### 3. Drawing-In or Entanglement Hazards

* **Description:** Occurs when loose clothing, long hair, gloves, or body parts become caught and pulled into moving machine components such as intermeshing gears, rollers, belts, or pulley drives.


* **Preventive Measure:** Tie back long hair, avoid wearing loose clothing or jewelry near rotating machinery, and fit protective enclosure guards over drive belts and pulleys.



#### 4. Impact Hazards

* **Description:** Caused when a worker is struck by a powered moving machine part or by flying objects/debris ejected from a machine during operation.


* **Preventive Measure:** Install protective shields or awareness barriers to contain flying chips/debris, and maintain proper machine component alignment to avoid structural collisions.

### Question 4 (3 Marks)

**Differentiate between hazard and risk.**

| Aspect | Hazard | Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | A source or situation with the potential to cause harm in terms of human injury, ill-health, or property/environmental damage. | The likelihood or probability that harm will actually occur, based on exposure to a hazard. |
| **Nature** | Refers to the inherent dangerous property or capability of an agent, object, or situation. | Refers to the probability of occurrence combined with the severity of adverse consequences. |
| **Relationship** | The existing condition or potential threat (e.g., electricity, chemicals, working at heights). | Calculated as the product of hazard and exposure ($\text{Risk} = \text{Hazard} \times \text{Exposure}$). |

### Question 5 (3 Marks)

**Mention any five hazardous properties of chemicals.**

1. **Toxic:** Chemicals that can act as poison or cause adverse acute or chronic health effects when they enter or contact the body (e.g., gasoline ingestion causing internal burns and organ distress).


2. **Explosive:** Substances that rapidly decompose to produce a large volume of expanding gas in an extremely short period (e.g., TNT, dynamite).


3. **Flammable:** Substances with low flashpoints that can easily ignite or burn when exposed to air or an ignition source.


4. **Oxidizing:** Chemicals that spontaneously release oxygen at room temperature or with mild heating, thereby initiating or intensifying the combustion of organic materials (e.g., peroxides, nitrates).


5. **Corrosive:** Chemical agents (acids or bases) that cause severe irreversible destruction or damage to living body tissues upon contact (e.g., hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide).

### Question 9 (7 Marks)

**Discuss about the suitability of fire extinguishers for different classes of fire.**

Fires are categorized into five distinct classes based on their fuel source, requiring specific types of fire extinguishers to extinguish them safely:

#### 1. Class A Fires (Ordinary Combustibles)

* **Fuel Source:** Solid combustible materials such as wood, paper, fabric, rubber, and plastics.


* **Suitable Extinguishers:**
* **Water & Water Mist Extinguishers:** Cool the burning material below its ignition temperature.


* **Foam (AFFF/FFFP) Extinguishers:** Blankets the fuel surface to cut off oxygen.


* **Multipurpose Dry Chemical (Ammonium Phosphate):** Melts and adheres to hot surfaces, forming a smothering coating.





#### 2. Class B Fires (Flammable Liquids & Gases)

* **Fuel Source:** Liquids and gases such as gasoline, alcohol, kerosene, oil-based coolants, and propane.


* **Suitable Extinguishers:**
* **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) Extinguishers:** Displaces oxygen and leaves no damaging residue.


* **Foam (AFFF/FFFP) Extinguishers:** Floats on liquid surfaces to secure the fuel and prevent reignition.


* **Dry Chemical (Ordinary/Multipurpose) & Clean Agents:** Interrupt the chemical reaction.




* **Unsuitable:** *Water must not be used* as it causes flammable liquids to spread.



#### 3. Class C Fires (Live Electrical Equipment)

* **Fuel Source:** Energized electrical wiring, panels, transformers, and appliances.


* **Suitable Extinguishers:** Non-conductive extinguishing agents such as **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)**, **Clean Agents (Halon Alternatives)**, or **Dry Chemical**.


* **Unsuitable:** *Water and conductive liquid agents must never be used* due to severe electrocution risk.



#### 4. Class D Fires (Combustible Metals)

* **Fuel Source:** Reactive metals such as magnesium, titanium, aluminum, potassium, sodium, and zirconium.


* **Suitable Extinguishers:** **Dry Powder Extinguishers** (applied via specialized discharge units or scoops) that absorb heat and smother oxygen.


* **Unsuitable:** *Water can react violently or cause explosions* when applied to burning metals.



#### 5. Class K Fires (Cooking Fats & Oils)

* **Fuel Source:** High-temperature cooking media such as vegetable oil, animal fat, and grease in commercial kitchens.


* **Suitable Extinguishers:** **Wet Chemical Extinguishers** (containing solutions of potassium acetate, carbonate, or citrate) which discharge a fine spray that saponifies the oil, forming a cooling foam blanket.

### Question 10 (7 Marks)

**Explain the standard procedure for HAZOP assessment.**

A **Hazard and Operability Study (HAZOP)** is a structured, systematic team methodology used to identify potential hazards and operability problems in process facilities before incidents occur.

```text
[Form HAZOP Team] ──► [Identify System Elements & Parameters] ──► [Consider Operating Variations] ──► [Identify Hazards & Failure Points] ──► [Generate Recommendations]

```

#### **Standard 4-Step HAZOP Procedure**

1. **Forming a Multi-Disciplinary HAZOP Team:**
* Assemble a team of experienced professionals with diverse technical expertise, including specialists from operations, maintenance, instrumentation, and process design/engineering.


* Members must possess deep system knowledge and a structured approach to evaluate process deviations.




2. **Identifying System Elements and Operating Parameters:**
* Break the complete process down into discrete sections or nodes using Piping & Instrumentation Diagrams (P&IDs) or plant models.


* Define design parameters for each element, such as normal flow rate, operating pressure, temperature, liquid level, and viscosity.


3. **Considering Possible Variations / Deviations:**
* Apply standardized guide words (e.g., *More*, *Less*, *None*, *Reverse*, *As Well As*) to each parameter to evaluate potential process deviations.


* Analyze causes and outcomes of deviations (e.g., *"What happens if pressure becomes unexpectedly high at this valve?"* or *"What if a valve opens too late?"*).




4. **Identifying Hazards and Failure Points:**
* Determine whether a process variation introduces a safety hazard or operational constraint.


* Estimate the severity/impact of the potential failure, evaluate the effectiveness of existing safeguards (alarms, relief valves), and determine the likelihood of occurrence.





#### **Results and Actionable Outcomes**

Following the review, the HAZOP team produces a documented stream of recommendations to improve system integrity, including:

* Modifying or adding safety relief systems and alarms.


* Installing or re-engineering emergency ventilation systems.


* Updating operational procedures or increasing sampling/testing frequency.


* Requesting detailed secondary reviews by specialized engineers.
